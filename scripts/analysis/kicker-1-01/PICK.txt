5. HOW LONG EACH ASSET LASTS
==============================================================================
the guarantee runs for his entire career. the pick's surplus runs for
the rookie deal, after which you pay the market for the same player.

  seasons as a real starter, careers observed start to finish:
    kickers (10+ attempts a year, 55 careers): median 5, 75th 9, 90th 13, max 18
    quarterbacks (100+ plays a year, 137 careers): median 2, 75th 5, 90th 7, max 19

  conditioning both groups on being good, since a leg that never
  misses is never cut and the median of all comers is not the
  relevant number:
    kickers, top quartile of career make rate (10 careers): median 10, 90th 17, max 18
    quarterbacks, top quartile of career EPA per play (11): median 10, 90th 17, max 19

  so the durability edge is real but much smaller than the raw
  medians suggest: good quarterbacks last a long time too.

6. SIDE BY SIDE
==============================================================================
  perfect kicker, vs an average leg     : +0.94 wins a season, guaranteed
  perfect kicker, vs a replacement leg  : +1.14 wins a season, guaranteed
  1.01 quarterback, mean of 1999-2021   : +1.28 wins a season, on the rookie deal
  1.01 quarterback, median              : +1.59

  share of 1.01 quarterbacks who beat the kicker over five years: 59%
  over a career: 0.94 x 10 seasons for a good kicker = 9.4 wins,
  against 1.28 x 5 = 6.4 wins of rookie-deal surplus
  from the average 1.01 quarterback. That is the strongest form of the
  case for the kicker, and it still needs the quarterback's post-rookie
  seasons to be worth nothing, which they are not - you keep the player
  and pay him, and he is still better than the alternative.
