1. THE MISMATCH, AND THE FIX
==============================================================================
the comparison leg, two rungs:
  AVERAGE     : the league's own make curve, 85.1% inside 60
  REPLACEMENT : the mid-season fill-in. Kicker-seasons of 5-20
                attempts (994 attempts) made 79.5% against 85.9%
                expected, a miss rate of x1.46

the quarterback is priced against a REPLACEMENT quarterback, so the
second row below is the like-for-like comparison. The first row is the
practically relevant one, because a team that passes on this kicker
signs an average leg that afternoon, while a team that passes on the
quarterback actually does play a replacement quarterback.

==============================================================================
2. WHAT THE GUARANTEE IS WORTH - ALL FOUR COMBINATIONS
==============================================================================
Two independent choices, so four cells. The PERFECT leg is in the
'with' term of all four; the rows are what gets subtracted, and the
columns are what coaching BOTH teams get.

                      real coaching  optimal coaching
what is subtracted                                   
vs an AVERAGE leg              0.84              0.63
vs a REPLACEMENT leg           1.13              0.72

  'real coaching' = what teams actually called, plus taking a
  guaranteed three when it beats that call. 'optimal coaching' = both
  teams take the best of kick/go/punt on every fourth down.

  Optimal coaching LOWERS the guarantee's value, because a well-coached
  team with an ordinary leg already kicks the 56-yarders and goes for it
  near the goal line. Better coaching substitutes for a better kicker.

  the components, real coaching:
    vs an AVERAGE leg     : kicks he takes  17.2 + kicks he unlocks  10.7 + XP  2.2 =  30.1 pts
    vs a REPLACEMENT leg  : kicks he takes  25.2 + kicks he unlocks  12.1 + XP  3.1 =  40.4 pts

  A NOTE ON 0.94, which appeared in earlier drafts: that figure did
  not subtract the part an ordinary leg would also have captured on the
  fourth downs he 'unlocks'. Correcting that gives the 0.84 above. Use
  this table; 0.94 is superseded.

==============================================================================
3. AGAINST THE 1.01
==============================================================================
                                    wins/season   certain?
  perfect leg, vs average leg             0.84        yes
  perfect leg, vs replacement leg         1.13        yes
  1.01 QB, vs replacement QB (mean)        1.28         no
  1.01 QB, vs replacement QB (median)        1.59         no

  like-for-like, both against replacement: 1.13 certain against 1.28 expected.
  That is a 12% edge to the quarterback on the mean, and 59% of the seventeen
  first overall quarterbacks since 1999 came in BELOW 1.13 over their
  rookie deal. The kicker's figure has no distribution at all.

  on the practical baseline: 0.84 against 1.28, and 35% of them came in below.

-- over a career, not a season
  kicker at 0.84 a year for 10 years vs average leg      :   8.4 wins
  kicker at 1.13 a year for 10 years vs replacement leg  :  11.3 wins
  average 1.01 quarterback, cumulative through year 10 :  11.7 wins

  On the like-for-like baseline that is 11.3 against 11.7 - a dead heat over a decade,
  with the kicker's half of it guaranteed and the quarterback's a draw
  from a distribution that includes JaMarcus Russell. On the practical
  baseline the quarterback wins clearly, 11.7 to 8.4.

==============================================================================
4. THE VERDICT, STATED HONESTLY
==============================================================================
  It depends on a baseline choice, and that should be said out loud
  rather than buried:

  - If passing on the kicker means signing an ordinary NFL leg -
    which is what actually happens, because average kickers are
    available on any Tuesday - he is worth 0.84 wins and the quarterback
    wins comfortably, 1.28 on the mean and 1.59 on the median.

  - If you insist on the strictly symmetric comparison, replacement
    against replacement, he is worth 1.13 guaranteed against 1.28
    expected, 59% of quarterbacks came in under him, and over ten
    years it is a coin flip. On that framing the risk-adjusted case
    for the kicker is real, not a joke.

  The first framing is the better answer to the question as asked,
  because the alternative to drafting a kicker is not fielding a
  replacement kicker. But the honest range for 'would you be mad' is
  'yes, and less than you would expect'.
