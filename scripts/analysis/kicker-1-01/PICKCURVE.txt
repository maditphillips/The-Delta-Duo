==============================================================================
4. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY
==============================================================================
  It says the EXPECTED return of a pick - averaged over everyone ever
  taken there - falls below the kicker inside the top ten. It does NOT
  say he beats every player taken after that: he beats the average
  outcome, and the spread around that average is enormous.

  Nor does it contradict the per-season finding that he loses to the
  1.01. Both are true because the top of the draft curve is very
  steep. Pick 1 returns about 12.4 career wins and pick 7 about 8.3;
  the kicker sits at 8.4. Per season that is 0.84 against the
  1.01 quarterback's 1.28 - a ratio of 1.52, which matches the career ratio of
  1.48. The two framings agree; they just answer different
  questions.

  Share of picks who never played a down for the team that drafted
  them: 4% in round 2, 12% in round 3, 51% after pick 150.

  And the calibration is fitted on quarterbacks, who are usually
  held to be UNDERSTATED by approximate value. That makes wins-per-AV
  too generous when applied to everyone else, which inflates the
  curve and pushes the crossover later. The real crossover is at
  least this early.
