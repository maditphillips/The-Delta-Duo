2. THE ELASTICITY: DOES OPPORTUNITY RESPOND TO KICKER QUALITY?
==============================================================================
682 team-seasons where the primary kicker had a record of 30+ prior attempts
prior_quality = career makes above expected per attempt, prior seasons only
  spread across those team-seasons: -0.057 (5th) to +0.071 (95th), sd 0.040
  a leg that never misses inside 60 would sit at +0.170, which is 4.3 standard deviations out

  team and season fixed effects, standard errors clustered by team.
  'per_sd' is the effect of a one-sd better kicker; 'if_perfect' is
  the same coefficient extrapolated to a leg that never misses.
                      outcome   coef     se      t     p  per_sd  if_perfect
            attempts per game  0.634  0.341  1.857 0.063   0.025       0.108
attempts per game + drives_pg  0.651  0.327  1.990 0.047   0.026       0.111
   50+ yard attempts per game  0.333  0.167  1.990 0.047   0.013       0.057
        mean attempt distance  3.247  2.360  1.376 0.169   0.129       0.552
contested-band kick rate (pp) 32.896 18.273  1.800 0.072   1.303       5.591
contested-band punt rate (pp) -6.671 21.804 -0.306 0.760  -0.264      -1.134

  read plainly: give a team a leg that never misses inside 60 and
  the observed elasticity predicts +0.11 field goal
  attempts a game and +5.6 points of contested-band kick
  rate. The case studies said +0.10 and +6.0. Same order of magnitude.

-- the league already moved further than any single kicker ever did
  contested-band kick rate, league: 17.8% in 1999 -> 48.7% in 2025
  contested-band punt rate, league: 52.1% -> 15.2%
  50+ attempts per game, league   : 0.174 -> 0.487
  Coaches respond to kicker quality, but they have responded far more
  to twenty-five years of the league getting better at kicking in
  general. The kicker-specific effect rides on top of that.

3. THE BREAK-EVEN: HOW MUCH EXTRA WORK WOULD HE NEED?
==============================================================================
the study window is 2018-2025: 4,454 team-games, 262 team-seasons
every fourth down inside the 42 that a team punted or went for, ranked by
how much a guaranteed make would have beaten the play they called:

   extra att/game  attempts/season  points/season  marginal pt/att    wins
             0.05              0.9            2.5             2.92    0.63
             0.11              1.9            5.0             2.48    0.70
             0.20              3.4            8.3             2.13    0.79
             0.30              5.1           11.0             1.62    0.87
             0.40              6.8           12.7             1.02    0.91
             0.54              9.2           13.7             0.38    0.94
             0.60              9.2           13.7             0.01    0.94

  the whole positive-gain supply is 0.54 attempts a
  game, 9.2 a season, worth 13.7 points.
  Past that, kicking is worse than the alternative and the marginal
  attempt costs points rather than adding them.

-- what the coach would actually give him
  regression elasticity              +0.11 att/game -> channel 3 =   4.9 points, total  24.9 = 0.70 wins
  case studies                       +0.10 att/game -> channel 3 =   4.7 points, total  24.7 = 0.69 wins
  optimal, what the study assumes    +0.54 att/game -> channel 3 =  13.7 points, total  33.6 = 0.94 wins

  So the headline 0.94 wins already assumes a coach five times more
  responsive than any real coach has ever been to a real elite kicker.
  Priced at the behaviour actually observed, he is worth about 0.70 wins.

-- so what would it take to beat the first overall pick?

  1.01 quarterback, mean: +1.28 wins = 45.8 points a season
    channels 1 and 5 give 19.9; the entire fourth-down
    supply gives 13.7; that is 0.94 wins and still 12.2 points short.
    a brand new possession that reaches the 42 and stalls is worth
    2.01 points, so he needs 6.1 more of them a season,
    +0.36 a game, on top of everything above.

  1.01 quarterback, median: +1.59 wins = 56.9 points a season
    channels 1 and 5 give 19.9; the entire fourth-down
    supply gives 13.7; that is 0.94 wins and still 23.3 points short.
    a brand new possession that reaches the 42 and stalls is worth
    2.01 points, so he needs 11.6 more of them a season,
    +0.68 a game, on top of everything above.

-- is that supply even there?
  fourth-down punts from just outside the guarantee (the 43 to the 60): 5,466
  = 1.23 a game. Those are the drives that would have to be
  pushed a few yards further to become kicks, and each one needs the
  offence to gain yards it did not gain. The supply exists; the
  conversion is not free, and none of it is in the 0.94.

-- stated as raw volume, and whether anyone has ever kicked that much
  to match the mean 1.01 quarterback: 1.94 + 0.90 = 2.84 attempts a game, 48 a season
    that is 8.3x the behavioural response the data actually shows
  to match the median: 1.94 + 1.22 = 3.16 attempts a game, 54 a season
    that is 11.4x the behavioural response the data actually shows

  the biggest field goal volumes any team has ever posted:

==============================================================================
SUMMARY
==============================================================================
  coaches DO respond to a better leg: +0.10 attempts a game in the case studies, +0.11 from the
  fixed-effects elasticity extrapolated to a perfect leg. The contested-band kick
  rate moves +6.0 and +5.6 points respectively.
  But the whole positive-value fourth-down supply is only 0.54 attempts a game
  (13.7 points), and the marginal attempt is worthless by the end of it.
  Priced at observed coaching behaviour he is worth 0.70 wins, not 0.94, and
  beating the 1.01 needs roughly eight times the response ever measured.
