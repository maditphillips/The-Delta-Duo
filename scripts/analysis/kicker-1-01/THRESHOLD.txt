  you should kick on almost anything.

  The boundary runs diagonally, and it does not collapse to one
  yards-to-go number:
    inside the 20 : go on 4th & 4 or less
    20 to 32      : go on 4th & 1 or 2
    32 to 38      : go on 4th & 1 only, and it is nearly a coin flip
                    (68.6% converted against 65.1% needed)
    38 to 42      : kick everything, 4th & 1 included - but 4th & 1 is
                    the closest call on the board (67.5% against 73.2%
                    needed, a 5.7 point margin)

  So yes: fourth and 1 is the one exception out at the edge of the
  guarantee, and from the 32 to the 38 you should still go for it.
  Only from the 38 to the 42, where the kick is 56 to 60 yards and the
  prize for converting is at its smallest, does a guaranteed three
  finally beat fourth and 1 - and it wins by a nose, not a mile.

  'go if togo<=' is the plain-English version of P*: the policy only
  changes at whole yards, which is why 42.9% and 45% are the same row.
  'FGA/gm' is fourth-down field goals a game. 'coach' is the policy
  run with an ordinary leg, against what teams actually called with an
  ordinary leg. 'leg' is the same policy run with the perfect leg minus
  the ordinary one, plus extra points and the off-fourth-down kicks.
  Both convert at 36 points a win. They add to the total.

-- what the sweep says
  your 3/7 rule, P* = 42.9%  (go if 4 or fewer to go):
    total +1.07 wins = +0.46 from the decisions + +0.61 from the leg
    1.38 fourth-down field goals a game
  best total          : P* = 50%, +1.15 wins
  the leg's share of that best row: +0.74 wins
  (the 'leg' column rises all the way to never-go-for-it, because the
   more you kick the more a guarantee is worth. That is not a
   recommendation - the total is worse there. The number that counts
   is the leg's share at the policy you would actually run.)
  go for it always    : -1.30 wins (-1.42 coach, +0.12 leg)
  never go for it     : +0.51 wins (-0.45 coach, +0.96 leg)

  Two separate findings, and they should not be blended:
  1. Fourth-down policy is worth up to +0.46 wins to any
     team with any kicker. That is free, and it is not the kicker's.
  2. The guarantee itself is worth 0.74 wins at that best
     policy. That is the number to compare against the 1.01.

  The total curve is flat across the middle - every threshold from 40%
  to 60% lands within 0.20 wins of the best - so your 42.9% is inside the
  plateau. The shortcut lands in about the right place despite the
  wrong premise. Both ends of the sweep are where it costs you.

3b. RECONCILING THIS WITH FINDINGS.txt AND RULE.txt
==============================================================================
Three scripts, three numbers for the same kicker. They differ only in
what the comparison team is allowed to do.

  channel 1, misses inside 60 become makes        :  17.3 points
  channel 3, perfect leg vs what teams really did :  12.4
    of which an ORDINARY leg would also have got  :   2.4  <- coaching, not kicking
    attributable to the guarantee itself          :  10.0
  extra points                                    :   2.1

  FINDINGS.txt   channel 1 + 3 + XP, baseline = real NFL coaching
                 = 31.9 points = 0.89 wins
  leg only       same, minus the part an ordinary leg would capture
                 = 29.4 points = 0.82 wins
  this script    both legs on the same optimal policy
                 = 26.5 points = 0.74 wins

  So the drop from 0.89 to 0.74 splits in two, and NOT evenly:
    0.07 wins is coaching an ordinary leg would also have captured.
      An average kicker attempting a 56-yarder already beats punting
      by 0.27 points; the guarantee only adds the 35-40% he misses.
    0.08 wins is the single-threshold rule paying for its own
      mistakes - it kicks on 4th & 4 near the goal line, where the
      surface in section 1b says go - and reshuffling which kicks he
      takes. Channel accounting never charges for a bad kick because
      of its max(0, .) floor.

  Honest range for the guarantee: 0.75 to 0.90 wins a season. Every
  route lands inside it, and none of them reach the quarterback.

4. SO WHAT IS THE WIN RATE?
==============================================================================
  the kicker's own contribution, which is what the 1.01 buys:
    your 3/7 rule, P* = 42.9%               : +0.61 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.1-7.9, 53.6%
    best threshold, P* = 50%                : +0.74 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.2-7.8, 54.4%
    no rule, best option every time         : +0.71 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.2-7.8, 54.2%

  and if you ALSO hand the team optimal fourth-down decisions, which
  they could have had for free with any kicker:
    both together                           : +1.25 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.8-7.2, 57.4%

  the first overall pick, for comparison: +1.28 wins a season on average
  (9.8 wins, 57.5%), +1.59 median (59.4%)

-- why the total is not the number to compare against the quarterback
  The total (+1.15 at the best threshold) is close to the
  average 1.01 quarterback (+1.28), but it is not the right
  comparison, because +0.41 of it is the fourth-down chart
  and any team can fix that with any kicker. Put both draft choices
  side by side and the chart appears on both sides and cancels:

    draft the kicker : 8.50 + 0.41 chart + 0.74 kicker = 9.65 wins
    draft the QB     : 8.50 + 0.41 chart + 1.28 QB     = 10.19 wins
    difference       : 0.54 wins a season, which is exactly QB minus leg

  The quarterback's +1.28 is that player against a replacement
  quarterback. It does not come bundled with a fourth-down chart
  either. Compare like with like: 0.74 against 1.28, or 1.59 at the median.
