  break-even P* = (FG - fail) / (convert - fail): the conversion rate
  going for it needs to beat a guaranteed three.

  The 3/7 shortcut says 42.9% everywhere. The real answer runs from
  38% at the goal line to 69% out at the 42, because that is
  where the prize for converting collapses relative to three points.
  Inside the 10 you should go for it on almost anything; out at the 42
  you should kick on almost anything.

  'go if togo<=' is the plain-English version of P*: the policy only
  changes at whole yards, which is why 42.9% and 45% are the same row.
  'FGA/gm' is fourth-down field goals a game. 'coach' is the policy
  run with an ordinary leg, against what teams actually called with an
  ordinary leg. 'leg' is the same policy run with the perfect leg minus
  the ordinary one, plus extra points and the off-fourth-down kicks.
  Both convert at 36 points a win. They add to the total.

-- what the sweep says
  your 3/7 rule, P* = 42.9%  (go if 4 or fewer to go):
    total +1.07 wins = +0.46 from the decisions + +0.61 from the leg
    1.38 fourth-down field goals a game
  best total          : P* = 50%, +1.15 wins
  the leg's share of that best row: +0.74 wins
  (the 'leg' column rises all the way to never-go-for-it, because the
   more you kick the more a guarantee is worth. That is not a
   recommendation - the total is worse there. The number that counts
   is the leg's share at the policy you would actually run.)
  go for it always    : -1.30 wins (-1.42 coach, +0.12 leg)
  never go for it     : +0.51 wins (-0.45 coach, +0.96 leg)

  Two separate findings, and they should not be blended:
  1. Fourth-down policy is worth up to +0.46 wins to any
     team with any kicker. That is free, and it is not the kicker's.
  2. The guarantee itself is worth 0.74 wins at that best
     policy. That is the number to compare against the 1.01.

  The total curve is flat across the middle - every threshold from 40%
  to 60% lands within 0.20 wins of the best - so your 42.9% is inside the
  plateau. The shortcut lands in about the right place despite the
  wrong premise. Both ends of the sweep are where it costs you.

3. WHAT A FIELD-POSITION-AWARE RULE BUYS YOU
==============================================================================
  take the best of the three options on every single fourth down,
  with no rule at all: +8.2 points, +1.25 wins
    of which coaching +0.55, the leg itself +0.71
  best single-threshold rule: +1.15 total, +0.74 leg
  the cost of using one number instead of thinking: 0.10 wins total, -0.03 of it the leg's

4. SO WHAT IS THE WIN RATE?
==============================================================================
  the kicker's own contribution, which is what the 1.01 buys:
    your 3/7 rule, P* = 42.9%               : +0.61 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.1-7.9, 53.6%
    best threshold, P* = 50%                : +0.74 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.2-7.8, 54.4%
    no rule, best option every time         : +0.71 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.2-7.8, 54.2%

  and if you ALSO hand the team optimal fourth-down decisions, which
  they could have had for free with any kicker:
    both together                           : +1.25 wins
                                              8.5-8.5 becomes 9.8-7.2, 57.4%

  the first overall pick, for comparison: +1.28 wins a season on average
  (9.8 wins, 57.5%), +1.59 median (59.4%)

  The honest comparison is leg against quarterback: +0.71 at absolute
  best, against +1.28 for the average 1.01 and +1.59 for the median.
  The kicker loses. He only draws level if you credit him with the
  fourth-down decisions too, and those were free all along.
