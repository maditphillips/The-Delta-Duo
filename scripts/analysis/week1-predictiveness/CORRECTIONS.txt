## WHAT CORRECTION 2 DOES TO THE READING, POSITION BY POSITION
   WR is the only position where scaling leaves the pattern intact under both
   rank measures: the gap in tier-sd units climbs from +0.02 at the top to
   +0.44 at 41+ under ECR, and +0.27 to +0.60 under the long-history proxy.
   RB holds under ECR (-0.25 to +0.51) but goes flat under the proxy (+0.69
   at the top, +0.56 at 41+), so the RB result rests on the thinner sample.
   QB is mixed: it climbs to the 13-24 tier then falls back at 25-40.
   TE does not resolve. Its raw ppg gap is largest at the top; in tier-sd
   units under ECR it also runs highest at the top (+0.60, +0.67) and lowest
   at 41+ (+0.25); but under the long-history proxy the same column runs the
   other way (+0.54 at the top, +0.67 at 41+). Three views, three answers, on
   27 to 178 players per cell. Nothing about the TE tier pattern is settled
   here, and its log-rank interaction is the weakest of the four (p = 0.042).

   The honest summary: scaled to each tier's own spread, the effect is well
   established at WR, likely at RB, unresolved at QB and TE. In raw points
   per game the split looks starker than that, because the later tiers score
   so much less that the same gap is a bigger share of their output.
