1. THE ONE-LINE ANSWER
   Averaged over every position and metric, a Week 1 opportunity number carries r=0.50
   to the rest of the season. A Week 1 efficiency number carries r=0.16.
   Week 1 tells you about ROLE. It tells you almost nothing about SKILL.

3. IS WEEK 1 SPECIAL, OR IS IT JUST ONE GAME?
   Running the identical test on weeks 2-9 instead of Week 1:
     QB: mean r from Week 1 = 0.284, mean r from a mid-season week = 0.305  (edge to Week 1: -0.022)
     RB: mean r from Week 1 = 0.445, mean r from a mid-season week = 0.445  (edge to Week 1: -0.000)
     WR: mean r from Week 1 = 0.399, mean r from a mid-season week = 0.414  (edge to Week 1: -0.015)
     TE: mean r from Week 1 = 0.455, mean r from a mid-season week = 0.475  (edge to Week 1: -0.020)
   Across all 55 position-metric pairs the average edge is -0.013.
   Week 1 is not a special window into a new season. It is one game, priced as one
   game. Roster churn and new schemes do not make it more informative than Week 6.

6. TEAM LEVEL
   1-0 teams: 54.0% win rate the rest of the way, 54% reach the playoffs
   0-1 teams: 46.4% win rate the rest of the way, 25% reach the playoffs
   r(Week 1 point margin, rest-of-season margin per game) = 0.251  (R2 = 0.063)
   The Week 1 win itself is worth about 1.2 extra wins over the remaining schedule --
   most of the 1-0 / 0-1 playoff gap is the game already in the bank, not a
   revealed change in team strength.
